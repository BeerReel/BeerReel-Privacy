BeerReel Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 August 2026

BeerReel is a social app for logging the drinks you have and sharing them with your friends. This policy explains what information the app collects, how it is used, who can see it, and the choices you have. BeerReel is operated from the United Kingdom and is the controller of the personal data described here. Please also read our Terms & Conditions.

Information we collect

  • Account details — your email address, display name, chosen handle, your profile photo if you set one, and the invite code your account is given. Your password is stored only as a salted cryptographic hash; we cannot read it. Password reset codes are stored the same way.
  • Content you post — photos you take in the app, the drinks you log (drink, rating, serving and measure, notes, and optionally the pub you're at), buddies you tag, and drinks you repost.
  • Social activity — your buddy connections and requests, accounts you have blocked, "cheers" you give and receive, posts you are tagged in, events you create, host, co-host or attend, and invite codes you issue or redeem.
  • Your taste profile — the categories and flavours you pick when you set your taste preferences, and the profile we work out from the drinks you rate. We use this to recommend drinks and pubs.
  • Location while the app is open — if you grant location permission, BeerReel asks your device where you are so it can suggest the pub you're in, place a new pub on the map, and centre the Map of Legends. This happens only while the app is open, never in the background, and your coordinates are not stored. Choosing a pub does record where and when you drank: that is part of your drinking history and is kept.
  • Push notification token — if you allow notifications, a device token is stored so we can notify you about buddy requests, tags, cheers, reposts and events.
  • Safety records — reports you make about other people's content, reports other people make about you, and any warning, suspension or ban recorded against your account, with an internal note explaining it.

Photos

Photos are resized and re-encoded on your device before they are uploaded, so the metadata a camera normally writes into a photo — including any location tag — does not reach us.

We store a storage key, never the image bytes and never a permanent link. Photos are shown through signed links that stop working after an hour, and the bytes travel from your phone straight to our storage provider without passing through our server.

If you choose to keep a shot in your camera roll, that copy is on your own device and is nothing to do with us.

How we use your information

  • To operate the app: running your feed, showing your posts to the people entitled to see them, keeping your drinking history, and running the events you attend.
  • To compute statistics — some shown only to you, some shown to your buddies and to people you have been at an event with, and some, such as the Map of Legends, shown to everyone. See "Who can see your content".
  • To recommend drinks and pubs you have not tried.
  • To send you notifications you have opted into, and to email you a code if you forget your password.
  • To keep the app safe: filtering offensive language, handling reports, and acting on breaches of our Terms.
  • We do not sell your data, show advertising, or use third-party tracking or advertising SDKs. There is no analytics or crash-reporting SDK in the app at all.

Our lawful bases

  • Performance of a contract — creating and running your account and the features you use.
  • Legitimate interests — keeping the app safe and usable: moderation, preventing abuse of invite and event codes, and keeping the shared catalogue accurate.
  • Consent — the camera, your location, and push notifications. Your device asks for each one, and you can withdraw any of them in your device settings at any time.

Who can see your content

Visibility in BeerReel is not a single setting. It depends on how you and another person are connected.

  • Your buddies — your posts in the feed for the last seven days, and your full profile: your statistics, top styles and drinks, your streak and your beerios.
  • Anyone who knows your handle — your profile photo, display name, handle and the date you joined, and nothing else. No statistics, no posts. Someone you have blocked cannot see even this.
  • People at an event with you — an event is joined with a six-character code, and anybody holding that code can join. While the event runs, attendees see each other's posts in the feed even if you are not buddies. The event album is permanent: it outlives the party, and everyone still at the event keeps it. People you have been at an event with can also see your full profile and statistics, including your average weekly units, for as long as you are both still in that event. Leaving an event, or being removed from it, takes your photos out of everyone else's album and takes the album away from you.
  • Everyone — the Map of Legends shows the most frequent drinker at each pub over the last 90 days. If that is you, your handle, display name and profile photo appear on a map that every BeerReel user can see. The names of drinks, breweries and pubs you add to the shared catalogue are also visible to everyone; your posts are not.

A host can appoint co-hosts, who are able to add drinks, remove guests, and rename, move or extend the event. Only the host can end it.

Blocking is mutual and immediate: neither of you sees the other anywhere in the app.

Where pub and drink information comes from

BeerReel's pub list is not typed in by its users. It is imported from public data, and two licences require us to tell you so.

Pub names and locations come from OpenStreetMap. © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0.

Addresses, postcodes and whether a pub is still trading are checked against the Food Standards Agency's food hygiene rating data. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

The ODbL asks that the pub rows we derive from OpenStreetMap are available under the same licence, so they are: osm-pubs-odbl.csv. It is regenerated each month when the import runs.

The coastline the Map of Legends draws its territory against comes from Natural Earth, which is in the public domain.

The drinks catalogue is written by us rather than licensed from anyone. Drink, brand and brewery names are used to identify what you are drinking and remain the property of their owners.

None of this involves your data. These sources describe pubs and drinks, and the licences above run to those rows and nothing else.

Automated review of drinks and pubs you add

If BeerReel doesn't have a drink, brewery or pub, you can add it, and it joins a review queue. Once a week that queue is worked through automatically: the details you submitted, together with your handle, are sent to Anthropic's Claude API, which searches the web and then approves the entry — correcting and filling in fields such as style, ABV and description — rejects it, or leaves it for a person to decide.

Nothing else is sent. Your check-ins, photos, feed, buddies, email address and location are not part of this.

What this decides is whether an entry joins the shared catalogue. It is not a decision about you and has no legal or similarly significant effect on you. If something you added was rejected and you think that is wrong, email us and a person will look at it.

Where your data is stored

BeerReel uses reputable infrastructure providers to store and process data on our behalf:

  • Neo4j Aura — database hosting (account, posts, social and event data)
  • Render — application server hosting
  • Cloudflare R2 — photo and map-tile storage; photos are accessed only via short-lived signed links
  • Expo — push notification delivery and over-the-air app updates
  • Google — sends password reset emails over Gmail's SMTP service. It is the only email BeerReel sends.
  • Anthropic — the automated catalogue review described above, and nothing else
  • GitHub — runs the scheduled import and review jobs, and hosts the page you are reading
  • Apple — distributes the app

These providers process data solely to provide their services to BeerReel. Several of them are based in the United States; those transfers rely on the UK extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or on standard contractual clauses.

Data retention and deletion

Your data is kept while your account exists. You can delete your account at any time in the app: the You tab → Account details → Delete account.

Deletion permanently removes your account, your sessions and your drinking history, and disassociates your photos, which become permanently inaccessible. You can also remove the photo from any individual post at any time, which deletes it outright.

Some things deliberately survive. Reports made about an account are kept as a record of what was decided rather than deleted. The drinks, breweries and pubs you added stay in the shared catalogue, because other people are using them. And events you attended carry on for the other attendees, though your own photos leave the album with you.

Security

Passwords and password reset codes are stored only as argon2 hashes. Sign-in tokens last seven days and are held in your device's secure storage. Photo links are signed and expire after an hour, and image bytes never pass through our server. All traffic is over HTTPS.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have the right to access, correct, export, or erase your personal data, and to object to or restrict its processing. Most of this you can do directly in the app; for a copy of your data, or for anything else, contact us at the address below and we will respond within one month.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint.

Age

BeerReel is about alcoholic drinks and is intended for users of legal drinking age (18+ in the UK), and you confirm you are 18 or over when you sign up. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you believe someone under 18 has an account, tell us and we will remove it.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, we will note the new date at the top of this page and inform you in the app.
